Hupy and Abraham in AI search.

We asked ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Google’s AI Overviews 20 questions a Milwaukee crash victim would ask, then compared how each one talks about Hupy and its competitors.

Hupy leads on ChatGPT. It trails on Google’s AI, and when Gemini and Google sort firms by case type, they send the serious injuries elsewhere.

If Hupy isn’t on that list, or is on it with the wrong reason attached, the TV and billboard work never gets its turn. Analytics won’t show the loss either, because nobody clicked anything.

Humans and bots

About half of any site’s traffic isn’t a person.

Bots made up 51% of all web traffic in 2024: 37% bad bots and 14% good ones, such as search crawlers (Imperva, 2025). Only a firm’s own server logs show its exact split, so the automated share below is estimated at that rate.

One signal is specific to Hupy. In October, 12.2% of Hupy’s desktop visits came from India and 10.8% from the Philippines. For Habush the figure was 8.4%, all from India. Hupy takes cases in Wisconsin, Illinois and Iowa, so those visits are very unlikely to be prospective clients. They are more likely vendors, offshore staff or automated traffic. Filtering them out would give a truer conversion rate.

Hupy already has the proof. Its website has 179 case-result pages, including $500,000 recovered for victims of a Milwaukee County bus crash and $1.6 million after an apartment fire. Across all 75 answers, the AI cited Hupy’s results once. Most answers cited the Milwaukee office page or the homepage, so the AI’s picture of the firm comes more from size, advertising and reviews than from results.
